Seasonal Energy Management

Energy use and solar production change throughout the year. Adjusting habits and settings with the seasons keeps bills low year-round.

Summer:
- Air conditioning drives summer usage. Set the thermostat to 78°F when home and use ceiling fans to feel cooler.
- Pre-cool during midday solar hours and coast through the afternoon peak.
- Close blinds and curtains on south- and west-facing windows during the hottest part of the day. Shading can cut cooling costs by up to 25%.
- Avoid using the oven on hot afternoons. Grill outdoors or use a microwave, air fryer or slow cooker instead.
- Solar production is highest in summer due to long days, so this is the best season to shift loads into daytime hours.
- Run pool pumps during the solar window in summer, and reduce daily run time to 6-8 hours with a variable-speed pump.

Fall:
- Schedule an HVAC tune-up before heating season and replace the air filter.
- Seal drafts around windows, doors and outlets with weatherstripping and caulk before cold weather arrives.
- Shorten pool pump run times as water temperatures drop, and close the pool for winter if possible.
- Days get shorter, so solar windows narrow. Recheck automation schedules that depend on solar timing.

Winter:
- Set the thermostat to 68°F when awake and lower it at night and when away.
- Open curtains on sunny south-facing windows during the day for free solar heat, and close them at night to retain warmth.
- Solar production can be 40-60% lower than in summer. Rely more on off-peak overnight hours for EV charging and laundry.
- Use space heaters only to heat a single occupied room for short periods, never as a whole-home heating solution.
- Lower the water heater to 120°F; heat loss from tanks and pipes is greater in cold weather, so insulate exposed pipes.

Spring:
- Spring is often the most efficient season: mild temperatures mean little heating or cooling, and solar production rises quickly.
- Open windows for natural ventilation on mild days instead of running HVAC.
- Clean solar panels after winter and check production against last year's spring output.
- Review the past winter's energy bills to identify which upgrades, such as insulation or a heat pump, would give the best return.

Year-Round Planning:
- Compare your monthly usage with the same month last year rather than the previous month, since seasons strongly affect consumption.
- Time-of-use rates and peak periods may change by season. Check your utility's summer and winter schedules.
